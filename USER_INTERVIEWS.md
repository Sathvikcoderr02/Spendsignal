# User Interviews

## Interview 1

**Name / role / stage:** A.K., Founder, B2B SaaS startup (12 people, seed stage)

**Quotes**

- “We pay the AI bill every month but don’t review it deeply.”
- “I need a tool that tells me what to change now, not just dashboards.”
- “If savings are real, I will book a call. If it feels salesy, I leave.”

**Most surprising thing**

They said trust mattered more than total savings number. Even if savings were only $150/month, they would still use the tool if it clearly explained the logic.

**What this changed in my design**

I made the output more honest for low-savings cases and avoided pushing fake opportunities. I also added a tool-level rationale line so users can understand the math path for each recommendation.

**Notes**

Interview lasted ~14 minutes over a call. The founder cared most about fast “what should I do next” actions and disliked pages with too much marketing text.

## Interview 2

**Name / role / stage:** R.S., Engineering Manager, dev-tools startup (28 people, Series A)

**Quotes**

- “We have Cursor, Copilot, and ChatGPT Team. I am sure we have overlap.”
- “Seat waste is our biggest issue, not API usage.”
- “If your report has a public link, I can share it in leadership chat.”

**Most surprising thing**

They were less interested in email reports and more interested in sharing a public report internally to get team approval.

**What this changed in my design**

I prioritized the shareable report URL flow and made sure the public page strips private details like email/company identity. I also added clean Open Graph/Twitter metadata so shared links look clear.

**Notes**

Interview lasted ~12 minutes through LinkedIn DM + follow-up call. This user wanted simple language and no finance jargon. They preferred one strong number at top: monthly savings.

## Interview 3

**Name / role / stage:** M.P., Indie hacker, solo founder (pre-revenue)

**Quotes**

- “I don’t need enterprise advice, just tell me if I am wasting money.”
- “Don’t block me with login before showing value.”
- “If savings are small, I still want reminders when plans change.”

**Most surprising thing**

They said a “no login, value first” flow is a major trust signal. They often close tools that ask for email before showing results.

**What this changed in my design**

I kept the flow as value-first: show full audit before email capture. I also added a low-savings message that says spending is already good and offers a “notify me later” angle instead of forced consultation.

**Notes**

Interview lasted ~15 minutes in a founder Discord voice channel. They wanted fast completion (under 3 minutes) and said too many fields would reduce completion rate.
